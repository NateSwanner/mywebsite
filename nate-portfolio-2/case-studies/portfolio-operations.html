Portfolio Operations & Growth Transformation

Built a repeatable operating model across a 22-property digital portfolio, bringing cost control, quality standards, cross-functional ownership, growth strategy, and revenue accountability into one scalable program.

Challenge

When I took ownership of the portfolio, costs were not tightly controlled, publishing was inconsistent, quality control was limited, and individual sites lacked clear operational ownership across functions such as sales and engineering. Monetization was underdeveloped, growth strategy was inconsistent, and there was no clear point of contact for outside partners and representatives.

I set an internal 90-day target to demonstrate measurable traffic improvement while building an operating model that could make each business line more sustainable and revenue-positive.

My Role

I owned the program end to end: planning, roadmapping, resource allocation, hiring, budgeting, P&L management, internal and external communications, quarterly reporting, process design and iteration, governance, outside vendor management, and execution oversight.

Approach

I created a standardized operating model for the portfolio, including a scalable editorial calendar that helped control cost, allocate capacity, improve quality, and create clearer expectations for staff and freelance contributors.

I worked with ad operations to improve monetization, partnered with sales teams to align content strategy with commercial priorities without compromising reader value, and developed distribution relationships and workflows with platforms including Flipboard, Google, and Apple to expand audience reach beyond traditional search.

As the first brands launched and matured, I identified repeatable growth patterns and operating signals that showed when a site was performing as expected and when intervention or a strategic pivot was needed. Those lessons became a reusable framework for later launches.

Governance & Delivery

  • Weekly team meetings for priorities, blockers, and coordination
  • Bi-weekly sprints for editors
  • Ongoing working sessions and individual guidance as projects progressed
  • Asana-based project tracking across brands for day-to-day visibility
  • Monthly leadership reviews covering P&Ls, performance, opportunities, risks, blockers, and required decisions
  • Quarterly reporting for broader business performance and planning

Risk & Dependency Management

The primary business risk was commercial viability: if brands did not grow quickly enough, they risked becoming ongoing cost centers rather than sustainable business lines.

A second risk was balancing editorial relevance with commercial goals. Content needed to serve readers first while also creating meaningful opportunities for sales and advertising teams.

Monetization introduced another tradeoff: revenue channels needed to be optimized without degrading user experience or weakening brand trust.

Engineering and design were critical dependencies during new brand and site launches, while leadership alignment was required on which concepts moved forward and how they were positioned.

Change Management Example

One brand launched without a sufficiently clear vision from leadership, creating confusion among stakeholders and the team. After roughly one month of operating the business line, I determined that the original positioning was unlikely to produce the desired results quickly enough.

I reframed the brand and content strategy, aligned the team around the new direction, and led execution against the revised vision. The brand began showing stronger growth soon after the pivot.

Lasting Impact

The most important result was not a single traffic or revenue metric. The program created a repeatable operating model for launching and managing digital brands.

By identifying common growth patterns, performance signals, governance needs, resource requirements, and intervention points during the first launches, I created a framework that made later launches faster to manage, easier to evaluate, and more scalable across the portfolio.
