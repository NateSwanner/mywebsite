Multi-Brand Content Operations & Workflow Transformation

Replaced an opaque, high-cost agency workflow with a scalable in-house operating model that improved quality, shortened cycle time, stabilized costs, and created capacity for faster growth.

Challenge

The existing content-production model relied heavily on an outside agency. Costs varied significantly, and the team had little visibility into how writers were sourced, how research was conducted, or how work moved through the agency’s process.

Assignments typically consisted of little more than a title and desired word count. Writers received limited context, which led to additional rewrites and editing costs. Turnaround was approximately one month, making the model poorly suited to news, fast-turn content, or rapid shifts in audience demand.

My Role

I identified the opportunity, mapped the existing process, designed the replacement workflow, researched the freelancer market, built the implementation plan, secured leadership approval, and communicated the change to affected teams.

I also directed workload and capacity planning, set priorities, assigned improvement work, monitored execution, and iterated on the system after launch.

Workflow Redesign

The new model created direct working relationships with freelance writers and gave editors much more control over assignment quality and production flow.

I developed a standardized article brief so each freelancer received the context needed to produce stronger work on the first pass. Editors made a deeper upfront investment in the Google Sheets editorial calendar, documenting assignment details, context, due dates, category priorities, and writer workloads.

We reviewed each site’s categories alongside traffic data and monetization opportunities to determine where content investment should be concentrated. The completed editorial calendar was then incorporated into Asana for execution and tracking.

Capacity & Prioritization

Capacity planning happened at both the writer and category level. Instead of distributing work based only on volume targets, we considered individual writer workload, subject-matter fit, category needs, traffic performance, and monetization potential.

Routine content production ran through the standardized editorial workflow, while I used a weekly sprint-style cadence for additional improvement work editors needed to complete outside normal publishing responsibilities.

Pilot & Rollout

Before moving fully away from the agency model, I tested the workflow with several writers who had worked with a sister publication and also contributed to our sites.

That pilot helped us fine-tune the Asana workflow and validate that the process made sense from the writer’s perspective. Once the model was clear and usable, we moved the broader operation onto the new system.

Change Management

Leadership’s largest concern was that directly managing freelancers could increase costs. I had researched market rates in advance and used that analysis to demonstrate that the new model was financially viable. The opposite ultimately occurred: direct sourcing significantly reduced production costs and made the expense category more predictable.

There was also concern that the team would struggle to manage the volume of incoming work. The solution was deliberate process design rather than simply adding more people.

Risk Management

The main transition risk was disruption to publishing volume while the new writer network and workflow came online.

The portfolio ran somewhat light on content for approximately one week, but the transition did not create a material operational interruption. By piloting the process before the broader switch and maintaining clear assignment visibility, we avoided the quality, capacity, and delivery problems leadership had been concerned about.

Results

~35% improvement in per-author productivity
Faster turnaround versus the prior month-long agency cycle
Lower and more predictable content-production costs
Fewer unnecessary rewrites and preventable editing
More editor capacity for quality and improvement work
+50% content production approved within the first 60 days

Lasting Impact

The briefs, editorial-calendar structure, capacity planning, Asana workflow, direct freelancer relationships, and improvement cadence all remained in place and continued to be iterated over time.

The transformation created a scalable system in which better planning produced better writer inputs, better inputs reduced cycle time and editing burden, stronger content improved traffic and monetization, and those gains could then fund additional production and growth.
